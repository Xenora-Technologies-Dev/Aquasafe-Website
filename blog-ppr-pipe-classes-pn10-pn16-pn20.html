Understanding PPR Pipe Classes: PN10 vs PN16 vs PN20 – Which One to Choose?

Pipe Selection Technical Guide April 14, 2026 7 min read

When selecting piping systems for plumbing, HVAC, or industrial applications, PPR (Polypropylene Random Copolymer) pipes are widely preferred due to their durability, corrosion resistance, and long service life. However, one of the most important decisions during selection is choosing the correct pressure class (PN rating). The most commonly used classifications are PN10, PN16, and PN20 — but what do these ratings actually mean, and how do you decide which one is right for your project?

What Does PN Mean in PPR Pipes?

PN stands for "Pressure Nominal", which indicates the maximum pressure (in bar) a pipe can safely handle at 20°C water temperature over a 50-year design life.

PN Pressure Ratings at a Glance
PN10 10 bar pressure rating @ 20°C
PN16 16 bar pressure rating @ 20°C
PN20 20 bar pressure rating @ 20°C
Important: As temperature increases, the allowable working pressure decreases. PN ratings are based on standard reference conditions (20°C). Always apply temperature derating factors for hot water systems.

PN10 PPR Pipes – Light Duty Applications

PN
10
PN10 – Light Duty
10 bar · Thin wall · Most economical
Key Characteristics
  • Pressure rating: 10 bar
  • Thinner wall thickness
  • Lightweight and cost-effective
Advantages
  • Most economical option
  • Easy installation
  • Suitable for low-pressure residential use
Limitations
  • Not for hot water systems
  • Not for high-rise buildings
  • Limited mechanical strength

Best Suitable For: Cold water supply, low-pressure domestic plumbing, gravity-fed systems, and non-critical distribution networks.

PN16 PPR Pipes – Standard All-Rounder

PN
16
PN16 – Standard Performance
16 bar · Medium wall · Most widely used
Key Characteristics
  • Pressure rating: 16 bar
  • Medium wall thickness
  • Balanced performance
Advantages
  • Versatile and widely used
  • Good cost-to-strength ratio
  • Suitable for most building services
Limitations
  • May not suit very high-rise pressure zones
  • Not ideal for extreme industrial pressure

Best Suitable For: Hot and cold water systems, residential and commercial buildings, HVAC chilled water (light duty), and pumped water distribution.

PN20 PPR Pipes – Heavy Duty High-Pressure Systems

PN
20
PN20 – Heavy Duty
20 bar · Thickest wall · Maximum strength
Key Characteristics
  • Pressure rating: 20 bar
  • Thickest wall section
  • Highest mechanical strength
Advantages
  • Maximum safety margin
  • Long-term durability
  • Ideal for demanding applications
Limitations
  • Higher cost
  • Heavier installation effort

Best Suitable For: High-rise buildings, booster pump systems, industrial piping networks, high-temperature hot water applications, and high-pressure mechanical systems.

PN10 vs PN16 vs PN20 – Quick Comparison

Feature PN10 PN16 PN20
Pressure Rating 10 bar 16 bar 20 bar
Wall Thickness Low / Thin Medium High / Thick
Mechanical Strength Basic Standard High
Relative Cost Low Medium Higher
Hot Water Suitability Not Recommended Yes ✔ Yes ✔
High-Rise Buildings Not Suitable Moderate Ideal ✔
Typical Application Cold water, low pressure General plumbing & HVAC High-rise & industrial

How to Choose the Right PN Class?

Choosing the correct PPR pipe class depends on several engineering factors:

System Pressure
  • Gravity systems → PN10
  • Pumped domestic systems → PN16
  • Booster / high-rise → PN20
Temperature Conditions
  • Cold water → PN10 or PN16
  • Hot water systems → PN16 or PN20
Building Type
  • Villas & low-rise → PN10 / PN16
  • Commercial → PN16
  • High-rise towers → PN20
Safety Margin
  • Account for water hammer
  • Pump starting pressure peaks
  • Future system expansion

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Don't Make These Selection Errors
  • Choosing PN10 for hot water systems — it is not rated for elevated temperatures
  • Using PN16 in high-rise booster pump lines without pressure calculations
  • Ignoring temperature derating factors when converting PN ratings to hot water applications
  • Selecting based only on cost instead of actual design pressure and temperature requirements
  • Failing to account for water hammer and pressure surges in selection

Final Thoughts

Selecting between PN10, PN16, and PN20 PPR pipes is not just a pricing decision — it is an engineering decision that directly affects system safety, efficiency, and lifespan. For reliable long-term performance, always match the pipe class with the actual design pressure, temperature conditions, and system type.

PN10
Economical, low-pressure & cold water use
PN16
Standard choice for most applications
PN20
High-performance & critical pressure systems
